# Experiment WMCS-EXP-0010

Status: pilot complete; accepted transition continued in EXP-0011

## Question

Can the public hostapd 802.11k/v interface measure one real client and issue a
single advisory transition only when a multi-sample source-signal gate says the
current AP is weak, without exposing station identity or forcing a disconnect?

## Provenance

- Researcher: local WMCS development session
- Date and timezone: 2026-09-20, Europe/Moscow
- Interface source: public hostapd and OpenWrt source/documentation
- Clean-room role: platform capability and policy experiment
- Data handling classification: sanitized local evidence

No roamd binary, source, configuration, trace, constant, or behavior capture
was used.

## Topology and initial state

- Globitel BT-RB300 controller: `192.168.1.1`, 5 GHz channel 149.
- Cudy TR3000 v1 agent: `192.168.1.2`, 5 GHz channel 40.
- One POCO F6 running Android 16 associated to the Cudy AP.
- Each AP had exactly one runtime Neighbor Report entry for the other AP.
- Runtime 802.11k/v was enabled by
  [`tools/lab/roaming-runtime.sh`](../../tools/lab/roaming-runtime.sh).
- No station MAC address or WLAN credential was collected by the workstation.

## Client capability observation

The phone advertised all of the following on its current Cudy association:

- 802.11v BSS Transition Management;
- 802.11k Neighbor Report;
- link measurement;
- passive Beacon Measurement;
- active Beacon Measurement;
- beacon-table measurement;
- LCI measurement;
- FTM range reporting.

Civic-location measurement was not advertised. The initial source RSSI was
-58 to -59 dBm.

## Beacon Measurement probes

One beacon-table request and one short active request were directed at the
single advertised Globitel neighbor. The active duration was 50 TU. Both
request frames were acknowledged at the 802.11 layer (`ack=1`), but the phone
returned no Beacon Measurement Report. Therefore no candidate RCPI/RSNI value
was available to policy. The experiment did not retry or treat the missing
report as evidence that the candidate was better.

These were 802.11k measurement requests, not BTM transition requests.

## BTM safety gate observations

The intended BTM request is advisory only:

- `disassociation_imminent=false`;
- `disassociation_timer=0`;
- one candidate Neighbor Report;
- `abridged=true`;
- validity period 30.

The first two BTM attempts stopped at the source-signal gate:

1. A single-sample preflight read -65 dBm against a -67 dBm threshold and
   aborted. A concurrent passive series ranged from -66 to -76 dBm, showing
   that one sample is too volatile for this decision.
2. A five-sample gate then observed five readings at -44 dBm against a -68 dBm
   threshold and aborted. The concurrent passive series subsequently moved
   through -55 to -62 dBm.

Both aborts happened before the hostapd BTM method was called. The second gate
confirmed `bss_transition_request_tx` delta 0 and
`bss_transition_response_rx` delta 0.

After the phone was moved toward the Globitel, a third five-sample window read
-72 to -68 dBm, with all five samples at or below the -68 dBm threshold and a
mean of -69.2 dBm. The gate passed and exactly one advisory request was sent.
Hostapd counters increased by one request and one response. The phone returned
BSS Transition status 1 (unspecified rejection), did not select the requested
target, and remained associated to the Cudy throughout the concurrent
25-second observation. Source RSSI during that observation ranged from -64 to
-72 dBm.

No retry was sent after the rejection.

## Post-rejection consistency check

Read-only checks after the response established that:

- the sole Neighbor Report stored on the Cudy exactly matched the live own-BSS
  Neighbor Report generated by the Globitel;
- both APs exposed the same SSID;
- both generated hostapd configurations used WPA2, the same
  `SAE WPA-PSK WPA-PSK-SHA256` AKM set, and optional PMF;
- both APs were enabled;
- the Globitel candidate was on channel 149, operating class 129, while the
  Cudy source was on channel 40, operating class 128.

These checks exclude a stale candidate record and an obvious advertised
profile mismatch. They do not prove that the phone could hear the candidate
well enough or that its local transition policy considered it suitable.

## Candidate radio fault found after rejection

A fresh Wi-Fi reconnect performed beside the Globitel still selected the Cudy
at -60 to -68 dBm. The passphrase/SAE material matched on both generated
hostapd configurations, and the Globitel recorded no client authentication
attempt. Read-only radio inspection then found the actual candidate fault:

- the Globitel's 5 GHz managed Wi-Fi-uplink station was associated on channel
  149 at 80 MHz;
- the same physical radio's home AP was configured as HE160;
- `iwinfo` reported channel 0 and an unknown center channel for the AP;
- the kernel repeatedly rejected an extension channel as disabled;
- channels 169, 173, and 177 needed by that upper 160 MHz placement were
  disabled in the active regulatory domain.

The ubus `ENABLED` state and generated Neighbor Report therefore overstated
candidate usability. This is a platform-adapter lesson: WMCS must validate the
live interface channel/width and recent radio errors, not only hostapd object
existence.

## Controlled candidate repair

The original `/etc/config/wireless` was retained on the Globitel as
`/tmp/wmcs-exp0010-wireless.before` with SHA-256
`e36a8a7e03267ed54eba8121a49bba15cbd7f7fec33a0033c9bfd671e82bf9fc`.
Only `wireless.radio1.htmode` changed, from `HE160` to `HE80`; the 5 GHz radio
was then reloaded.

Post-action verification showed:

- live AP channel 149, width 80 MHz, operating class 128;
- hostapd status `ENABLED`;
- zero new extension-channel-disabled errors during the verification window;
- the Cudy and its 2.4 GHz WDS management path remained reachable;
- runtime k/v was restored with exactly one fresh remote Neighbor Report per
  AP and no additional UCI mutation by the runtime helper.

No second BTM was sent after the repair because the rejected request remained
in cooldown. A 15-second passive observation still showed the existing phone
association on the Cudy at -63 to -69 dBm; that is not evidence against the
repaired candidate because the phone had not performed a fresh scan.

A subsequent fresh Wi-Fi reconnect beside the Globitel selected the repaired
Globitel AP immediately. Eight passive samples showed one client on the
Globitel at -46 to -54 dBm and zero clients on the Cudy; the association still
advertised both BTM and Neighbor Report support. This proves that the repaired
candidate is visible and usable by the POCO F6. Together with the pre-repair
fresh association choosing Cudy, it is strong evidence that candidate radio
failure caused the earlier status-1 rejection, although the unspecified status
code cannot prove the client's internal reason by itself.

## Post-repair passive walk

With the phone initially associated to the repaired Globitel, a 120-second
privacy-preserving walk was observed without sending any BTM. Every sample
showed one client on the Globitel and zero on the Cudy. Source RSSI ranged from
-36 to -72 dBm, including multiple isolated readings at or below -70 dBm, but
the POCO F6 did not roam naturally. This is direct sticky-client evidence and
also confirms why policy must require a window rather than react to one weak
sample.

## Reverse run excluded from BTM evidence

During preparation for the reverse Globitel-to-Cudy request, the first passive
sample already contained entries for the same station on both APs: a constant
-77 dBm entry on the Globitel and a strong -42 dBm entry on the Cudy. The
source-side gate looked only at hostapd presence, treated the old Globitel
entry as current, and sent one advisory BTM. The Globitel counter increased by
one request, but no response arrived.

Follow-up checks established that this was not a simultaneous association:

- a one-use salted identity comparison showed that both entries represented
  the same station without exporting its address;
- over three seconds, the Globitel entry transferred zero bytes in either
  direction while the Cudy entry transferred traffic in both directions;
- kernel inactivity was 171270 ms on the Globitel and 0 ms on the Cudy;
- the Cudy association had begun before the reverse request observation.

The phone had therefore roamed naturally before the request. The BTM was sent
to a stale source entry and cannot be credited for the transition. The run is
retained as negative controller/observer evidence, not roaming-success
evidence.

The Cudy was subsequently rebooted. Full wpad, the AP, and the 2.4 GHz WDS
management path survived; volatile Neighbor Reports reset as expected. The
runtime helper restored one current neighbor per AP without further UCI
change, and the reboot removed both stale client entries. A later clean sample
showed the POCO F6 active only on the Globitel at -50 dBm.

## Observer correction

[`tools/lab/observe-roaming.sh`](../../tools/lab/observe-roaming.sh) now keeps
the raw hostapd station count but also reports kernel inactivity and a bounded
`recent_activity_count`. Kernel activity is aggregated from the complete
station dump instead of joining addresses: an MLO-capable client may use an
MLD address in hostapd and a link address in nl80211. Neither address leaves
the router. The activity field is diagnostic rather than proof of association,
because a legitimately quiet client can exceed the threshold.

## Interpretation

- Capability advertisement alone is not a steering trigger.
- Missing Beacon Reports must be tolerated and cannot be converted into a
  fabricated target score.
- An instantaneous RSSI gate is unstable; WMCS needs a bounded sample window,
  hysteresis, and cooldown.
- Aborting before mutation/request is working as intended.
- A valid, acknowledged advisory BTM may still be rejected by client policy;
  WMCS must record the response, enter cooldown, and leave the association
  untouched.
- A transition result and interruption measurement are still pending and must
  not be called seamless.

## Next action

Keep the rejected direction in cooldown. Start a separately identified reverse
run with the POCO F6 associated to the repaired Globitel, walk toward the Cudy
without toggling Wi-Fi, require a weak multi-sample Globitel window, and send
at most one advisory BTM toward the already proven Cudy. The send gate must
reject a stale source entry using recent kernel activity, not only hostapd
presence. Capture the response and association timeline; do not turn a
rejection into a forced disconnect. The resulting accepted compatibility run
is recorded as [EXP-0011](WMCS-EXP-0011-accepted-advisory-btm.md).
